# Collecting Brand Materials

How to guide the user in gathering brand materials for discovery, and what to look for in each category.

## What to Ask For

Walk the user through the categories below and accept whatever they can provide. Even one strong source (a style guide, a pitch deck, or a handful of customer-facing emails) is enough to start.

### 1. Official brand documents
- Style guides, brand books, voice and tone guidelines
- Messaging frameworks, value proposition documents
- Naming and terminology standards

### 2. Sales and marketing content
- Pitch decks, one-pagers, product pages
- Email templates, LinkedIn posts, sales scripts
- Press releases, launch announcements, case studies

### 3. Customer-facing communications
- Support responses and knowledge-base articles
- Newsletters and lifecycle emails
- Website copy and landing pages

### 4. Conversational samples (reveal implicit voice)
- Call transcripts and meeting notes
- Slack or Teams threads where the team discusses customers or strategy

### 5. Design materials
- Design system docs (color, typography, spacing)
- Brand asset libraries and presentation templates

## Where Materials Typically Live

Users often don't know where their brand materials are. Common locations to prompt them to check:
- Shared drives (Google Drive, OneDrive, SharePoint, Box)
- Wiki or knowledge base (Confluence, Notion, Guru)
- Their own inbox — forwarded templates, decks, and past campaigns
- Marketing or sales enablement folders

## Tips

- **Ask for the best examples first**: "What's the one deck or email you'd want a new hire to read first?" — highest-signal materials come out quickly.
- **Recent beats old**: deprioritize materials older than ~12 months unless they're explicit brand documents.
- **Check for conflicts**: if the user provides materials from different eras or teams, note where they disagree — that's a discovery finding, not an error.
- **Name the company**: get the company name up front so the discovery report is grounded in real positioning.
